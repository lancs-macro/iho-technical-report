\section{Platform and Outputs}
\label{sec:website}

\subsection{Overview}

The Observatory publishes at \url{https://int.housing-observatory.com/}.
The platform has four public outputs --- an interactive dashboard, a
quarterly monitoring report, a nowcast report, and the Housing Fever
companion site --- all driven by the same quarterly estimation pipeline
(Section~\ref{sec:data-access}) and all served from the same data API
(Section~\ref{subsec:api}).

\subsection{Interactive Dashboard}

The dashboard is the platform's primary interface. It presents, by economy:

\begin{itemize}
  \item BSADF sequences plotted against their critical values, with the
        periods classified as explosive shaded;
  \item real house price and price-to-income series with the same episodes
        overlaid, so that a statistical episode can be read against the
        movement in the underlying series;
  \item tables of ADF, SADF and GSADF statistics against critical values at
        the 90\,\%, 95\,\% and 99\,\% levels;
  \item date-stamp tables giving the start, peak, end and duration of each
        detected episode;
  \item cross-country views summarising exuberance status across the panel
        for a given quarter.
\end{itemize}

Users can switch between the two indicators --- real house prices and the
price-to-income ratio --- and inspect the aggregate series alongside
individual economies. The dashboard is built in Shiny and reads its inputs
from the data API, so it reflects whatever release has most recently been
processed.

\subsection{Quarterly Monitoring Reports}

A PDF monitoring report accompanies each release. Its structure is
consistent across quarters: an executive summary, a review of global price
and affordability developments, per-economy detail, and comparative sections
placing the most recent real house price and real income developments side by
side across the panel.

The reports are where the statistical output is given economic reading. A
date-stamped episode in a single country is of limited interest on its own;
what the reports add is context --- how the current episode compares in
duration and intensity with that country's own history, and whether it is
isolated or part of a synchronised movement across economies, as in the
early-2000s boom, the 2008--09 bust, and the 2020--22 pandemic-era surge.
Reports are archived by quarter on the platform.

\subsection{Nowcast Reports}

The nowcast report at
\url{https://int.housing-observatory.com/nowcasting.html} publishes the
output of the model in Section~\ref{subsec:nowcast}: the current-quarter
estimate with bootstrap confidence intervals, the transformed input series,
in-sample fit, and the rolling out-of-sample RMSE comparison against the
random walk and AR(1) benchmarks. Reporting the validation alongside the
nowcast is deliberate --- a point estimate for a quarter that has not yet
been published is only interpretable next to evidence on how well the model
has done at the same task in the past.

\subsection{Companion Platform: Housing Fever}

Housing Fever, at \url{https://housing-fever.com/}, publishes the PSY-IVX
decomposition of Section~\ref{subsec:psyivx}. For each economy covered it
shows the observed price-to-rent ratio against its estimated fundamental
path, the non-fundamental residual, and the episodes date-stamped in each.

The division of labour between the two sites follows the division between
the methods. The main platform answers whether prices are behaving
explosively, for the full panel. Housing Fever asks whether that behaviour is
accounted for by fundamentals, for the smaller set of economies where the
required rent and interest rate data support the question.

\subsection{Open-Source Software}

The estimation pipeline is built from packages that are themselves public,
so that the Observatory's results can be reproduced independently
(Table~\ref{tab:software}).

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Open-source \textsf{R} packages used by the Observatory}
  \label{tab:software}
  \begin{tabular}{lp{7cm}p{3.2cm}}
    \toprule
    Package & Purpose & Reference \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{ihpdr}
      & Retrieve IHPD releases and archived vintages
      & \citet{ihpdr} \\[4pt]
    \texttt{exuber}
      & Recursive right-tailed unit root tests; simulated critical values;
        date-stamping
      & \citet{vasilopoulos2022} \\[4pt]
    \texttt{ivx}
      & IVX estimation with persistent regressors, used in the PSY-IVX
        decomposition
      & \citet{ivx} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent \texttt{ihpdr} and \texttt{exuber} are on CRAN
(\url{https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/ihpdr/},
\url{https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/exuber/}); development versions
of all three are on GitHub under \url{https://github.com/kvasilopoulos}.
