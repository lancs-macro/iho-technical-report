\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The Observatory applies two complementary econometric frameworks to detect
and date-stamp periods of housing market exuberance.

% ── 4.1 GSADF ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{The GSADF Test}
\label{subsec:gsadf}

The \textit{Generalised Supremum Augmented Dickey--Fuller} (GSADF) test
\citep{phillips2015a, phillips2015b} is a recursive, right-tailed unit root
test designed to detect mildly explosive autoregressive behaviour in time
series. It extends the earlier SADF test of \citet{phillips2015a} with
improved power for detecting multiple explosive episodes within a single
sample. The test has been applied to international house prices by
\citet{pavlidis2016} and \citet{pavlidis2018}.

\subsubsection{Estimation}

For sub-sample $[r_1, r_2] \subset [0, 1]$ (fractions of the full sample),
the test fits an ADF-type regression:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:adf}
  \Delta y_t = \alpha_{r_1, r_2}
             + \beta_{r_1, r_2}\, y_{t-1}
             + \sum_{j=1}^{k} \phi_j\, \Delta y_{t-j}
             + \varepsilon_t,
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the lag order selected by an information criterion. The
null hypothesis is $H_0\colon \beta = 0$ (unit root) against the
right-tailed alternative $H_1\colon \beta > 0$ (explosive root).

The GSADF statistic is the supremum of the $\mathrm{ADF}_{r_1}^{r_2}$
statistics over all feasible sub-sample pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gsadf}
  \mathrm{GSADF}(r_0) = \sup_{\substack{r_2 \in [r_0, 1] \\
                                        r_1 \in [0,\, r_2 - r_0]}}
                        \mathrm{ADF}_{r_1}^{r_2},
\end{equation}
where $r_0$ is the minimum window size. Critical values are obtained by
Monte Carlo simulation.

\subsubsection{Date-Stamping: the BSADF Sequence}

The \textit{Backward SADF} (BSADF) sequence enables date-stamping of
exuberance episodes. For each end-point $r_2$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bsadf}
  \mathrm{BSADF}_{r_2}(r_0)
    = \sup_{r_1 \in [0,\, r_2 - r_0]} \mathrm{ADF}_{r_1}^{r_2}.
\end{equation}
A period $t$ is classified as \textit{explosive} when
$\mathrm{BSADF}_{r_2}$ exceeds the right-tailed critical value at the
chosen confidence level (95\,\%, 90\,\%, or 80\,\%).

\subsubsection{Application to House Price Data}

The Observatory applies the GSADF test to two series for each country:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RHPI} --- real house price index (inflation-adjusted).
  \item \textbf{RHPI/RPDI ratio} --- house-price-to-income ratio
        (affordability-adjusted).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Software.}
The \texttt{exuber} \textsf{R} package \citep{vasilopoulos2022} implements
GSADF estimation, Monte Carlo critical values, and BSADF date-stamping:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={GSADF estimation with \texttt{exuber}}]
install.packages("exuber")
library(exuber)

result <- radf(price_series)
cv     <- radf_cv(nrow(price_series))
datestamp(result, cv)
\end{lstlisting}

% ── 4.2 PSY-IVX ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{The PSY-IVX Approach}
\label{subsec:psyivx}

The PSY-IVX method \citep{shi2021} decomposes the price-to-rent ratio into
fundamental and non-fundamental (speculative) components. Unlike the GSADF
test, which examines prices in isolation, PSY-IVX explicitly models the role
of economic fundamentals in driving house prices.

\subsubsection{Two-Step Procedure}

\paragraph{Step 1 --- Fundamental component estimation.}
The long-run relationship between the price-to-rent ratio and economic
fundamentals (interest rates, income, user cost of capital) is estimated
using IVX estimation \citep{shi2021}, which is robust to persistent and
potentially explosive regressors.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- Exuberance testing.}
Recursive right-tailed unit root tests of the PSY type are applied to the
non-fundamental residual from Step~1. Explosive dynamics in the residual
indicate speculative pricing that is decoupled from fundamentals. PSY-IVX
results are published at \url{https://housing-fever.com/}.
